Enterprise product design

Turning performance records into decisions

I redesigned a text-heavy management module to help leaders identify work that needed attention and understand patterns without relying on manual spreadsheet analysis.

Role
Product Designer
Focus
Research, prototyping, validation
Users
Frontline to regional managers

Overview

A useful record system that demanded too much interpretation

The Performance Discussion Form module gave managers access to important employee records, but its dense, text-based table made priority work difficult to recognize at a glance. The information was there; finding and interpreting it required time and concentration.

Historical analysis created a second burden. Managers exported records to Excel, maintained separate spreadsheets, and manually compared patterns across departments and time periods.

The challenge

Support the decision, not just the record

How might we reduce the effort required to find important discussions while helping leaders understand broader performance patterns inside the product?

01

Priority was buried in prose

Managers needed to read and interpret individual entries before they could judge urgency or severity.

02

Scale increased the burden

Leaders responsible for larger organizations had more records to scan before locating work that needed attention.

03

Trends lived elsewhere

Cross-department and historical comparisons required exports, separate spreadsheets, and manual tracking.

The users

One system, different levels of responsibility

Research included managers across the organization, from people closest to individual discussions to leaders looking across departments.

Direct scope

Frontline team managers

Managed individual contributors and worked closest to specific employee discussions and follow-up actions.

Team oversight

Second-level managers

Managed other managers and needed visibility across multiple teams and discussion records.

50–80 people

Regional managers

Looked across a region or department to understand priorities and broader organizational patterns.

Research approach

Observe, prototype, validate

We spoke with managers before and after prototyping so the design could respond to real work patterns rather than assumptions.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Contextual interviews

    Managers described the role of performance discussions in their day, the volume they handled, the actions they took, and the metrics they tracked.

  2. 02

    Observe

    Current-state walkthroughs

    Participants demonstrated the existing table and the external tools they used, revealing delays, workarounds, and missing analytical support.

  3. 03

    Test

    Clickable prototype

    Managers walked through the proposed experience while we observed navigation obstacles and opportunities to improve the interaction.

  4. 04

    Refine

    Follow-up validation

    After revising the prototype, we returned to participants for a focused check that the identified usability issues had been addressed.

Observed

Participants paused and scanned multiple entries when asked to find something requiring immediate attention.

Reported

Managers described the module as difficult to navigate and less capable than newer areas of the product.

Workaround

Managers maintained spreadsheets to compare patterns such as lateness discussions, PIP usage, and behavior-related discussions across departments.

Design direction

Two jobs emerged from one table

Operational

What needs my attention now?

Reduce the reading and interpretation required to locate important records, then make focused review easier.

  • Surface meaningful signals
  • Support focused filtering
  • Preserve access to record detail

Analytical

What patterns should I understand?

Bring common historical and departmental comparisons closer to the source data, reducing repetitive export work.

  • Compare departments
  • Review patterns over time
  • Adapt detail to management scope

Design principles

Make complexity easier to act on, not invisible

01

Lead with significance

Help managers recognize which records deserve attention before asking them to parse the full narrative.

02

Design for organizational scope

Keep individual record detail useful for frontline managers while supporting broader views for senior leaders.

03

Connect action with context

Pair immediate work with historical patterns so managers can move between a specific discussion and the larger picture.

Validation

Testing the workflow with the people who manage it

The final clickable prototype was tested with managers through guided walkthroughs. We observed where participants encountered obstacles, revised the experience, and returned for a focused follow-up review.

Reflection

The opportunity was not a prettier table

The research reframed this project from modernizing an outdated interface to supporting two kinds of management work: acting on individual discussions and learning from patterns across many of them.

What I would measure next

Time to identify a priority record, reliance on spreadsheet exports, and whether managers at different levels can find the right depth of information without losing context.
